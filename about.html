About the Museum

Built from a shipping warehouse, kept as a room for looking.

Meridian opened in 1974 in a disused grain warehouse on the north bank. Half a century later the building still shows its original timber frame, and the museum still runs on the same idea: fewer works, more room around each one.

Our Approach

We hang between forty and sixty works per gallery, roughly a third of what the space could hold. Wall text is kept short and placed to the side, so the first thing you meet is the work itself rather than a paragraph about it.

Every exhibition is accompanied by an open study room where the research files, loan correspondence and conservation notes are available to read. Visitors are welcome to sit there as long as they like.

The Building

The 1891 warehouse was converted by Aila Renner between 1971 and 1974. Her plan left the timber columns in place and lit the galleries entirely from above, through sawtooth roof glazing that faces north. On clear days no artificial light is used before four in the afternoon.

A second wing was added in 2009, holding the print study room, a 150-seat lecture hall and the conservation studio.

The Collection

Meridian collects in four areas: post-war painting, sculpture in wood and stone, darkroom photography, and artists' moving image. Around 280 works are on view at any time, and the rest is accessible by appointment through the study room.

A Short History

1971

The warehouse is bought

A group of eleven artists and two shipping families acquire the derelict grain store and commission its conversion.

1974

First galleries open

Three rooms open to the public with a founding gift of 310 works, most of them by artists living within the region.

1996

Photography archive arrives

The Harbour Lane negative archive, some 18,000 sheets, is transferred to the museum and placed in cold storage.

2009

North wing completed

The study room, lecture hall and conservation studio open, allowing the collection to be shown and researched in the same building.

2024

Fiftieth anniversary

A year-long rehang draws on works that had not left storage since the founding gift.
